A qualified flop or turn bluff or semi-bluff candidate bets or raises, in Hold'em and in PLO4, and nothing switches it off; an unqualified hand is never made to bluff by this layer (the river's owner instruction, Dan 2026-11-06, applied to every street: "CHECK THAT THERE IS NOTHING THAT IS EVER BLOCKING OR PRENENTING, OR 'SHUTTING OFF' ... BLUFFS FOR QUALIFIED CANDITATES OR SITUATIONS"). Real HorseLogic.decide on 1/2 cash single-raised pots against an opponent memory has never seen: a Hold'em open-ender c-bets, barrels and probes, two overcards barrel, and a flush draw check-raises, at non-zero bounded rates owned by the baseline (the open-ender barrel beats the no-baseline rate by 15 points, the check-raise by 10); a PLO4 wrap barrels and probes a blank turn (it bet 0 of 200 times before) and check-raises the flop, a nut flush draw c-bets, and no PLO bet exceeds the pot; junk decides exactly as without the layer, showdown value, a locked satellite seat and five-way pots are not qualified, a four-way pot without a draw is never bluffed and an all-in opponent never gets a baseline bluff; a station gets fewer semi-bluffs but never zero; a tournament bubble still semi-bluffs, no more than cash; and Dan's PLO paired-board rule holds (no semi-bluff raise into a paired Omaha board, half the betting frequency). A red law means a flop or turn bluff is again shut off for a qualified candidate, or a hand that should check is being made to bluff.
