# tests/a-reliability-signal-is-never-lost-to-a-lock.law.test.ts

The reliability controller learns that an alert stopped only from a resolved delivery, and an escalated incident leaves `escalated` only on one. On 2026-11-18 at 01:04 UTC the bulk alert closer and the financial-alerts backfill resolved alerts of incident 73c08234 (LightningHandHost.post_commit_obligations_pending) in two transactions at once. `reliability_apply_signal` locked each member row before the shared incident row, so the two transactions deadlocked member -> incident -> member. The intake trigger caught the 40P01, wrote the delivery to `smarter_private.reliability_intake_rejections` and moved on, and nothing ever read that table back. Two members stayed `firing` against alerts resolved hours earlier, and the incident kept paging for more than ten hours after its alert stopped. 97 deliveries were lost the same way in two days, every one to a transient lock error. This law pins three things. Every delivery of one incident takes one transaction lock on the incident fingerprint before any row lock, so that cycle cannot form. A transient rejection is replayed from its inbox row by `reliability_replay_rejected_intake`, the first step of every minute's `reliability_tick`, oldest first, through the unchanged `reliability_ingest_alert` (idempotent on inbox row, delivery count and status), with a capped backoff. A delivery that an observed newer delivery of the same record has overtaken is superseded, never replayed, so an old firing cannot raise a false page. `ReliabilityIntakeRejections` now pages only for a delivery the controller could not apply, not for a deadlock the next tick healed. Any later migration that redefines `reliability_apply_signal`, `reliability_tick` or the intake trigger must carry all of it.
